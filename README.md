# Roberto Cepeda Rocandio

Final-year student of the double degree in **Mathematics and Physics** at the University of Oviedo (Spain), with an Erasmus+ year at Sapienza University of Rome (2025–26).

**What I work on:** My interests lie in the mathematical foundations and computational methods of quantitative finance, particularly stochastic processes, changes of measure and derivative pricing. My work combines the study of the underlying theory with its application to financial models, from the construction of risk-neutral measures using Girsanov's theorem to Monte Carlo methods for option pricing. I am currently exploring variance reduction techniques and their extension to path-dependent options and rough volatility models.

### Research

- **Bachelor's thesis in Mathematics** · *From the Risk-Neutral Measure to Girsanov's Theorem in Stochastic Integration: Pricing of Financial Products* · defence expected May 2027 · [Spanish original and English translation](https://github.com/indexxxxbraker/option-pricing-via-martingales)
- **Bachelor's thesis in Physics** (in progress) · *Path Integrals in the Pricing of Path-Dependent Options* · defence expected May–June 2027 · [project overview](https://github.com/indexxxxbraker/path-integral-option-pricing)
- **Beca de Colaboración** (research grant, Spanish Ministry of Education, 2026–27) · *Changes of Measure in Stochastic Integration: Application of Risk-Neutral Measures to Financial Engineering* · Department of Statistics, Operations Research and Mathematics Education, University of Oviedo.                                                                    
Within it, I am extending importance sampling by a Girsanov drift, the subject of the project below, to path-dependent options and to rough volatility, starting with the rough Bergomi model, where volatility is driven by a fractional Brownian motion.

### Projects

- [**girsanov-importance-sampling**](https://github.com/indexxxxbraker/girsanov-importance-sampling) · Importance sampling by a Girsanov drift for out-of-the-money options under Black–Scholes: the optimal drift in closed form, every estimator proved and tested against exact values, and a pilot run that recovers the optimum. Python package, 225 tests and a 27-page note; the starting point of the research grant above.
- [**quant-finance-portfolio**](https://github.com/indexxxxbraker/quant-finance-portfolio) · Numerical methods for derivative pricing, from Black–Scholes to the Heston model, each pricer checked against an independent method. Following a review in October 2026, errors were corrected and the notes revised so that every numerical result is generated by a script in the repository. Python, with a C++17 port and 28 LaTeX notes.
